# A native SAE on the production space — can one lens replace two?

Question (2026-08-08): section 22 read the corpus through a second model
(gemma-2-2b + gemma-scope). The HypotheSAEs line (arXiv 2502.04382,
2408.00657, 2411.00786) says you can skip the borrowed model: train a small
top-k sparse autoencoder directly on the embeddings you already have and the
production space becomes natively interpretable. E2 tries that on the Qwen3
v2 space and measures what it costs.

Generated by `scripts/plot_native_sae.py`. Every number in
`experiments/sae_qwen/report.json`; the pipeline that produced it is in
`experiments/sae_qwen/`.

## Method

- **Data.** 16,483 deduplicated 1024-d vectors pulled read-only from the v2
  Chroma collections (11,412 segments, 4,726 memories, 345 videos), spanning
  5,026 notes. L2-normalized. 10% of *note keys* held out, so a video's
  segments never straddle the split.
- **Deviation from the brief.** The brief restricted memories to
  `interest.content_sources`. That subsets the corpus to 12,034 vectors and
  leaves 27% of the retrieval corpus out of distribution by construction —
  and the faithfulness gate scores retrieval over all of it. Training used
  all 16,483; the restriction was run as a measured ablation instead (below),
  and the content-source restriction is still where it belongs, on the
  note-level taste regression.
- **Model.** Top-k SAE in the Gao et al. 2024 form: pre-encoder bias,
  ReLU→TopK latents, **untied** decoder with unit-norm rows and the parallel
  gradient component projected out, encoder initialized to the decoder
  transpose, AuxK revival (256 dead latents, coefficient 1/32). Adam 3e-4,
  500-step warmup, batch 512, CPU (2x faster than MPS at this size — the
  launch overhead dominates).
- **Budget.** Held-out reconstruction is evaluated every 250 steps and the
  best-val state is kept. It has to be: at 4096 latents val peaks near step
  2,000 and then *decays* while train loss keeps falling. The sweep uses a
  4,000-step budget for comparability; the winning config was re-trained to
  its plateau (14,000 steps) for everything downstream.

## Config sweep — held-out reconstruction cosine, 3 seeds each

| dict | k | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | mean ± sd | dead % | L0 | FVU | train−val gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2048 | 16 | 0.7921 | 0.8108 | 0.8149 | 0.806 ± 0.010 | 0.00 | 16 | 0.449 | 0.096 |
| 2048 | 32 | 0.8135 | 0.8291 | 0.8322 | **0.825 ± 0.008** | 0.00 | 32 | 0.412 | 0.094 |
| 4096 | 16 | 0.7767 | 0.7965 | 0.8020 | 0.792 ± 0.011 | 0.08 | 16 | 0.477 | 0.131 |
| 4096 | 32 | 0.8021 | 0.8184 | 0.8202 | 0.814 ± 0.008 | 0.00 | 32 | 0.434 | 0.119 |

Wider is *worse*. 4096 latents overfit 15k training vectors: a bigger
train−val gap and an earlier val peak (mean best step 2,167 vs 4,000).
Dead features are a non-issue at this scale — AuxK keeps 99.9%+ alive
everywhere. L0 equals k exactly in every run.

| condition (2048, k=32, 14k steps) | held-out cos | in-distribution | out-of-distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| trained on all 16,483 | **0.829 ± 0.008** | 0.796 | 0.909 |
| trained on content sources only (12,034) | 0.763 ± 0.002 | 0.782 | 0.718 |

The restriction does not hurt the content vectors it was trained on
(0.796 → 0.782); it destroys the vault notes it never saw (0.909 → 0.718).
Note also that vault memories reconstruct *far better* than content — session
briefs and project notes are formulaic; transcript segments are not.

## Faithfulness — retrieval through an index of reconstructions

The 156 frozen eval queries keep their real Qwen query vectors; only the
index is reconstructed. The numpy mirror of the production ranking (videos
collapsed per video_id, memories per doc_id, merged by cosine; segments in
their own collection; rerank and reflected boost off) reproduces
`eval/retrieval/baseline.json` **exactly** — hit@1 .7115, hit@5 .9038,
hit@10 .9423, and every per-bucket cell. Nothing in production was changed
and no baseline was re-stamped.

| config | overlap@10 | top-1 agreement | hit@5 recon | Δhit@5 | Δhit@1 | Δhit@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2048, k=16 | 0.727 | 0.742 | 0.823 | −0.081 | −0.107 | −0.047 |
| 2048, k=32 | 0.782 | 0.816 | 0.855 | −0.049 | −0.068 | −0.024 |
| 4096, k=16 | 0.749 | 0.754 | 0.840 | −0.064 | −0.090 | −0.024 |
| 4096, k=32 | 0.787 | 0.801 | 0.878 | −0.026 | −0.066 | −0.026 |
| 2048, k=32, plateau | 0.798 | 0.833 | 0.863 | −0.041 | −0.049 | −0.019 |

Reconstruction cosine and retrieval faithfulness rank the configs
differently: 4096/k32 reconstructs worse than 2048/k32 but retrieves better.
A recon-cosine sweep alone would have picked the wrong dictionary.

The verdict on the number itself: one in five top-10 results changes, and the
gate's own regression tolerance is 0.02. A reconstructed index fails the
production gate at every config tested. The SAE is a readable side-channel,
not a drop-in replacement for the vectors.

## Are the features real?

Decoder directions, best cosine match against an independently trained run
(random-direction null: 0.107):

| comparison | mean max-cos | median | frac > 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 seeds, each with its own split | 0.415 | 0.334 | 9.4% |
| 3 inits on one **fixed** split | 0.413 | 0.337 | 8.5% |

Identical. The instability is not the data moving — it is the optimizer
landing somewhere else. At 16k vectors, ~91% of a 2048-latent dictionary is
not a reproducible object.

But the *head* is. Restricting to the top-100 latents by firing frequency —
the ones that got named — and requiring the match to hold against **both**
independent inits: mean max-cos 0.762, 50% above 0.8, versus 0.383 and 6.5%
for the dictionary as a whole. The frequently-firing latents are largely real
directions in the space; the long tail is fit noise.

Reconstruction quality, unlike the dictionary, is highly reproducible:
three inits on one split give 0.8171 / 0.8168 / 0.8163 (sd 0.0002), while
three different splits give 0.8171 / 0.8319 / 0.8368 (sd 0.0084). Seed
variance in the headline number is almost entirely *split* variance — which
of the 5,026 notes landed in the 10%.

## What the latents say

100 latents named by claude-haiku-4-5 from their 8 strongest activating
excerpts (95 high confidence, 5 medium; +10 regression survivors named
separately — 113 API calls total). **These are unprobed hypotheses.** No
latent here has been causally tested, and the 18.x drug-usage lesson applies
unchanged.

a granularity the Qwen theme partition never reaches ("Icon library migration
to Lucide" is one afternoon's work, not a theme). That is the strongest
argument for the native lens, and it is qualitative.

## The taste experiment

Note-level activations (mean over a note's own vectors), L1 logistic
regression, 5 repeats of stratified 5-fold CV, plus a shuffled-label null and
the same folds run on the raw mean-pooled Qwen note vector.

| target | n | positives | AUC | shuffled null | raw Qwen vector | sign-stable features |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — r≥1 vs r=0 (deliberate save) | 610 | 272 | 0.771 ± 0.047 | 0.497 | **0.978** | 11 |
| B — r≥2 vs r=1 (wrote a thought) | 272 | 27 | 0.661 ± 0.105 | 0.471 | 0.613 | 0 |
| C — B, Instagram only | 222 | 19 | 0.591 ± 0.113 | 0.474 | 0.544 | 0 |

**Target A is confounded, completely.** Every r≥1 note in this vault is
Instagram/TikTok/web/Pinterest and every r=0 note is YouTube, except 7
YouTube notes carrying a `## My take`. The deliberate-save label *is* the
medium label. A is a source classifier.

The confound is what makes A's numbers informative anyway: the raw Qwen
vector solves it at 0.978, the sparse code at 0.771. The bottleneck throws
away a fifth of an AUC on a signal that is trivially linear in the space it
was trained on. Interpretability here is not free.

The 11 sign-stable survivors of A read as YouTube-topic detectors (all but
one carry a negative coefficient, i.e. predict r=0): "Reference frames and
coordinate changes", "Search indexing and data retrieval systems",
"Endosymbiotic origin of complex cells", "Input specification and encoding",
"Pragmatic alternative approaches", "Variable assignment and mutation",
"Learning how systems work", "Microservice architecture and service
integration", "Rapid experimental iteration", "Adding dimensions solves
geometric problems"; the single positive is "Visual compositing and density
rendering". Read honestly, these name what he *watches* rather than what he
*saves* — they are the confound speaking.

Targets B and C hold the medium fixed and are the honest tests. Both clear
their shuffled nulls but neither produces a single feature whose coefficient
keeps its sign across all 25 folds, and B's ±0.105 spread swallows the gap to
chance. At 27 and 19 positives there is nothing to find. **The headline
output of the taste experiment is that this vault cannot currently support
it** — not because the SAE failed, but because the deliberate-save signal is
perfectly collinear with the capture medium.

## Caveats

- Small-data regime throughout: 16k vectors is ~2 orders of magnitude below
  where SAE dictionaries are normally trained, and the overfit peak, the
  wider-is-worse ordering, and the 9% dictionary reproducibility are all
  symptoms of it.
- Segment-vs-note shift: 69% of the training vectors are 60-second transcript
  segments, but the taste regression is note-level, built by mean-pooling a
  note's activations. Mean-pooling a top-k code is not itself sparse and has
  no principled justification beyond convenience.
- The 4,000-step sweep budget was mildly binding for the 2048 dictionaries
  (still climbing at the cutoff) and not for the 4096 ones (already
  decaying), so the sweep slightly understates 2048 relative to 4096. The
  plateau re-run (+0.004 for 2048/k32) does not change the ordering.
- Feature names come from one model reading 8 excerpts. Nothing is probed.
- The faithfulness mirror disables reranking; production runs the same way by
  default today, which is why the mirror matches the baseline exactly, but a
  future rerank-on default would make this measurement a lower bound.
- Only two of three fixed-split inits were run (plus the plateau s0 that
  shares that split), so the init-variance sd is from n=3.

## Verdict — can a native SAE replace the two-lens architecture?

Not as a replacement, clearly yes as an addition. The native SAE wins the
thing section 22 said the borrowed lens could not do: it reads *subject
matter* in the production space, at a finer grain than the theme partition,
with 95/100 names landing at high confidence and the frequently-firing half
of them reproducing across independent training runs. That is a genuinely
better instrument for "what is in this corpus" than a gemma-scope fingerprint
that turned out to encode register. But it cannot carry the space: an index
of reconstructions loses one in five top-10 results and fails the retrieval
gate by 2-4x its tolerance at every config, and the sparse code destroys a
fifth of an AUC on a note-level signal the raw vectors classify almost
perfectly. The honest architecture is not one lens or two — it is the
production vectors doing retrieval, unchanged, with a native SAE mounted
beside them as a read-only annotation layer over the head of its dictionary.
Whether that layer can answer taste questions is untested here, because the
only deliberate-save label this vault carries is a disguised source label.
